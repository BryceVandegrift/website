There is an idea that plagues a lot of modern thought that has been bugging me
recently. I often see this idea hidden in conversations that I have with
other people when discussing nature, reality, or sometimes just in normal
conversations. It's an idea that many of the people championing it don't
explicitly know that they are.

The idea that I am referring to is nominalism.

## What-ism?

The main premise of nominalism is quite simple: universals and abstract
objects don't actually exist within reality, but are just names for things[^1].
For example, a dog isn't a universal thing that exists within reality, but a
name for a creature that behaves and looks like what we expect a "dog" to be.
This may initially sound like a simple or even trivial thing, but it has very
wide reaching implications.

This means that to a nominalist there is no such thing as shortness, a mammal,
or a father in reality, they're only names for things. The nominalist does not
argue about or look to universals, but only particulars. There is no universal
triangle, only particular triangles. There is no humanity, there are only
humans.

One interesting thing that I find sometimes when I talk to people is that some
of them are unknowingly nominalist.
I recall talking to a coworker about the "nature" of something some time ago,
and this is kind of how the conversation went:

> Me: "This *thing* is a part of human nature."\
> Coworker: "Human nature isn't a real thing."\
> Me: "Oh, so you're a nominalist?"\
> Coworker: "What's a nominalist?"

I think that nominalism is kind of an implicit way of thinking that you find
within the modern world. In fact, I sometimes catch myself slipping into
nominalist-like thinking from time to time if I'm not paying close attention.
That is not to say that *everyone* is a nominalist,
but that it feels like it's a default way of thinking nowadays. Perhaps this
might be because materialism is so common today, and materialism is largely
(in my eyes) dependent on nominalism as it's foundation.

## The Problem

So why bring this up? Why talk about nominalism? Well you see...I have a
confession to make. I don't think nominalism is attainable as a view of
reality.

A problem that I have with nominalism is that it basically presupposes
universals.
In fact, some could say that nominalism is a universal that applies to
everything **except itself**. That every universal doesn't exist in reality
*except* the universal of nominalism. I don't know how to see this but as
self refuting.
I can't even make an argument *for* nominalism without using universals.
It seems as though it is heavily reliant on universals while also **denying**
the existence of them at the same time.

### Logic

Another massive critique of nominalism is that logic itself seems to just
fall apart. Let's take one of the most common syllogisms in logic:

```
A: All men are mortal.
B: Socrates is a man.
C: Therefore, Socrates is mortal.
```

Premise A, "All men are mortal" is what breaks down. What we're saying in
premise A is that mortality is a property that belongs to all humans. This is
**exactly** what a universal is. In fact, I can just rephrase premise A as:
"Mortality is a property of human nature". This is a massive practical
problem with nominalism as you can't really "group" things. I've tried to
"reason" my way out of this conclusion using the nominalist view and I simply
cannot find a way to do so.

While these two criticisms of nominalism are just criticisms of it in theory,
I tend to find more problems in practical or applied nominalist reasoning as
well.

## Realism

Now if there's one thing I greatly dislike, it's tearing down ideas without at
*least* proposing alternatives.
So if nominalism isn't a practical view of reality, then what is an
alternative? While there
are a few different ways to answer that, my answer would be realism.
Realism affirms that universals and abstract concepts exist within reality.
While there are different schools of realism, there's really only two big
ones that I know of: Platonic realism and moderate realism.

Now I won't dive into Platonic realism or moderate realism as I don't think I
will do a good enough job explaining the two. Besides, the point of this blog
post isn't even to provide a case for realism, but rather to provide a case
*against* nominalism.
If you want, you can just read
about realism elsewhere or I can write another blog post about it at some
point in the future.
But trust me when I say that **actually** affirming that universals exist is
100% more coherent than the alternative.
