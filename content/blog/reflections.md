**Note:**
This is a series of reflections that I wrote on Good Friday of 2025 (last
year). I cannot remember why I didn't publish this back then, so I waited
until this year to do so.

---

For those who are unaware, today is Good Friday[^1]. Good Friday is
traditionally a somber day of silence and reflection in remembrance of the
passion and death of our Lord. Today I've been reflecting not just on the
life and death of Christ, but also on my life and death.

There is an old saying that goes something like: "You die twice. Once when you
stop breathing and a second time, when someone utters your name for the very
last time." Although this saying is very much true, it honestly reminds me of
the fruitlessness of living to appease those around us. That is not to say
that it is **bad** to appease others, but rather it *is* a waste of time to
please people for the sake of pleasing them.

I've fallen into this trap in the past time after time. I've spent way too
much time trying to please everybody that I can. The problem with this is, if
you try pleasing everybody you end up pleasing **nobody**. It is **literally
impossible** to please everyone.
Not only that, but it will absolutely make you more miserable (trust me).
I have had to learn these lessons the hard way.
 
It goes without saying that one should do what is right rather than what is
pleasing to others. As Saint John Chrysostom put it:

> "If you knew how quickly people would forget about you after your death, you
> will not seek in your life to please anyone but God." --Saint John Chrysostom

Despite what I may think, death is closer to me than I expect. I truly don't
know when I will die. The time that I have left is dwindling day after day.
So what will I have done after my time here has come to an end?

As my death grows closer and closer I find myself relying more on the Lord
than anyone else in my life. My friends, heck even my **family** will come and
go throughout my life, but there is really only one who will not abandon me.

One question that I have been asking myself is: "When I die and look
back at my life, will I have lived to please those who will eventually forgot
me, or will I have lived to please the one who will
always remember me even unto the end of the ages?"
